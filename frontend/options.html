What this backtest holds

    First position
    Profit or loss at expiry, per unit

    • Direction (delta): the gain or loss from the underlying's moves, at the position's exposure that day.
    • Size of moves (gamma): how that exposure changed as the price moved. Option sellers lose here on big days; buyers gain.
    • Time decay (theta): the value options lose each day as expiry approaches. Sellers collect it; buyers pay it.
    • Volatility (vega): the change in implied volatility, each leg at its own strike and expiry.
    • Other: what a daily Greek split can't capture, mostly the last days before expiry, when the Greeks change fastest, and rate moves.
    Exposure over time

    A good backtest can be luck, and every setting you try is another chance to find some. This test runs the same strategy with 20 settings (days to expiry × exit rule) and asks two questions: does the best setting's Sharpe beat what the luckiest of 20 would show by chance (Deflated Sharpe), and does the setting that looks best in one half of the history still rank well in the other half (the probability of backtest overfitting, PBO)? It also reruns your own settings from five start dates a week apart, because which days a strategy happens to roll on can swing its result by many points.
